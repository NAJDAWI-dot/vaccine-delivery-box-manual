Introduction

The Cold Chain Problem in Jordan

Extreme heat, aging infrastructure, and paper-based tracking create a critical vaccine delivery gap. This Device is engineered to close it.

1.1 Purpose of This Manual

This manual provides complete guidance for the safe setup, operation, maintenance, and troubleshooting of the Enhanced Reliability Vaccine Delivery Box. It is structured for healthcare workers, logistics personnel, and facility managers responsible for last-mile vaccine delivery across Jordan's diverse geographic and climatic environments.

1.2 Project Background

Approximately 35% of vaccines worldwide experience temperature excursions during transport, resulting in potency losses of up to 15% and a global financial loss of $35 billion annually. In Jordan, extreme summer temperatures regularly exceeding 43°C, paper-based immunization records, and aging cold chain infrastructure create a critical reliability gap at the vaccine carrier level. This Device addresses that gap through an integrated suite of active cooling, smart access control, and real-time monitoring.

Safety Warnings & Precautions

Read Before Operating

Failure to comply with these warnings may result in vaccine spoilage, patient harm, or device damage. All users must read this section first.

Critical

Never use this Device to transport vaccines already flagged as temperature-compromised. The RFID lock prevents opening after an excursion — do NOT attempt to force the lock open.

Warning

Do not expose the Device to water immersion. The Device is splash-resistant but not waterproof. Keep all electronic components dry at all times.

Warning

The Peltier modules and heat sinks become hot during operation. Do not touch the external heat dissipation vents. Keep ventilation areas clear of obstruction at all times.

Caution

Only use the approved 12V lithium-ion battery pack supplied with the Device. Non-approved batteries may cause overheating, damage, or BMS failure.

Caution

The supercapacitor backup provides 15–30 minutes of emergency power only — it is not a replacement for a fully charged battery.

2.1 General Safety Rules

  • Always inspect the Device before each use for physical damage, seal integrity, and battery charge level.
  • Never modify or tamper with the circuit, RFID module, or locking mechanism.
  • Keep the Device away from direct sunlight during transport — use a reflective outer cover where possible.
  • Do not stack more than 3 units vertically during transport.
  • Operate only within the rated ambient range: −10°C to 50°C.
  • Follow all JFDA and WHO guidelines for vaccine handling at all times.

2.2 Vaccine Safety

  • Always pre-condition PCM coolant packs in a standard freezer for a minimum of 12 hours before use.
  • Never place vaccines in direct contact with PCM packs — always use the custom-cut foam holders as a buffer.
  • Log each delivery trip in the RFID system. Do not bypass the electronic traceability requirement.
  • If the Green LED is not lit at trip start, do not load vaccines into the Device.

Standard Operating Procedures

Delivery Lifecycle

From loading to delivery — the Device monitors and protects vaccines through every phase of the journey.

5.1 Loading Vaccines

  • 1Confirm Green LED is lit and Device has been pre-cooling for at least 30 minutes.
  • 2Tap your registered RFID card to unlock the Device.
  • 3Open the lid fully — it is hinged and will stay open.
  • 4Retrieve vaccines from cold storage. Handle vials gently — do not shake.
  • 5Place each vial into the designated custom-cut foam slot. Never stack vials outside the foam slots.
  • 6Ensure no vaccine contacts the PCM packs directly — always use foam holders as a buffer.
  • 7Verify quantity against the dispatch document before closing.
  • 8Close the lid firmly until both latches click into the locked position.
  • 9Tap your RFID card to electronically confirm closure and begin trip logging.
  • 10Record delivery details in the trip log form (Appendix A).
Critical

If the LED changes to Amber or Red during loading — STOP. Close the lid and wait 5 minutes. If it does not return to Green, escalate to your supervisor immediately.

5.2 During Transport

  • Monitor the LED status panel at every stop and every 30 minutes.
  • Keep the Device upright at all times — interior foam holds vaccines vertically.
  • Protect the Device from direct sunlight using a reflective insulated transport bag.
  • Keep the rear heat dissipation vents clear — do not press against vehicle seats.
  • If Red LED or flashing: follow the Emergency Protocol in Section 8 immediately.

5.3 Operating in Extreme Heat (Above 40°C)

  • Increase PCM pre-conditioning to a minimum of 14 hours (not 12) before deployment.
  • Limit trip duration to a maximum of 4 hours above 40°C ambient.
  • Use a white or silver reflective outer cover over the Device during transport.
  • Increase LED monitoring frequency to every 15 minutes.
  • Carry a spare charged battery for trips exceeding 3 hours in extreme heat.

Cooling System — Technical Details

Three-Layer Thermal Defense

Active Peltier cooling via aluminum cold plates (direct conduction) + passive PCM thermal buffer + supercapacitor emergency backup — three independent layers ensuring 2°C–8°C under any condition.

6.1 Primary Cooling: Dual Peltier Modules (TEC1-12706) with Copper Heat Pipe Loop

The Device uses two TEC1-12706 Peltier thermoelectric modules wired in parallel to the 12V power supply. Each module measures 40 × 40 × 3.6 mm and is rated at 12V / 6A (72W maximum input), with a maximum achievable temperature differential (ΔTmax) of approximately 66°C. At Jordan's peak ambient of 43°C, maintaining the compartment at 5°C requires a ΔT of ~38°C — well within the module's capability given the polyurethane foam insulation and active fan-cooled heat sinks.

The cold face of each Peltier is bonded with thermal paste to a copper cold block (the condenser end of the heat pipe loop). From there, 3–4 sintered-wick copper heat pipes run along the interior walls and floor of the vaccine compartment. Heat pipes are sealed passive two-phase devices: the working fluid (water) evaporates at the interior ends (evaporators) by absorbing heat from the compartment, travels as vapor to the cold block at the Peltier (condenser), re-condenses, and wicks back — completing a continuous passive loop with no pump and no moving parts. This distributes cold across the entire interior surface rather than a single point, giving far more uniform temperature than a single cold plate. The hot face of each Peltier is bonded to an external aluminum heat sink cooled by a 12V fan. The microcontroller uses MOSFET-based PWM switching to regulate both modules independently. The parallel wiring provides redundancy: if one module fails, the other continues operating.

Warning

The Peltier hot-side heat sinks should feel very hot during operation but must not exceed approximately 60°C. If the heat sink cannot be touched briefly (1 second), check immediately that the cooling fan is running and vents are unobstructed. Hot-side saturation severely reduces cold-side performance and can permanently damage the modules.

6.2 Passive Thermal Buffer: Phase Change Material (PCM)

The PCM packs are not the primary cooling source — that role belongs to the Peltier cold plates. Instead, the PCM packs mounted in the lid serve as a passive thermal buffer and emergency backup layer. During normal operation, they reduce the cooling load on the Peltier modules (lower PWM duty cycle, longer battery life) and absorb heat spikes when the lid is opened. During a power failure, the PCM transitions from solid to liquid while absorbing large amounts of latent heat, passively maintaining the interior below 8°C for an extended period with no power required — critical for Jordan's remote outreach areas where power is unreliable. Unlike ice packs, water-based PCM releases cold energy at a near-constant rate through the phase transition, eliminating the risk of freezing vaccines below 2°C as long as vaccines are not in direct contact with the packs.

6.3 Emergency Backup: Supercapacitor

The supercapacitor activates automatically if the primary battery voltage drops below threshold, providing 15–30 minutes of emergency power. It self-recharges from the primary battery during normal operation and requires no manual intervention.

RFID Smart Locking System

Smart Access Control Logic

Every access event is authenticated, logged, and linked to real-time temperature data — creating a complete tamper-evident audit trail.

7.1 Normal Unlock Procedure

  • 1Approach the RFID reader panel on the right side of the Device.
  • 2Hold your registered RFID card flat against the reader icon.
  • 3Wait for a single audible beep — both latches simultaneously release.
  • 4Open the lid within 10 seconds. If not opened, latches re-lock automatically.
  • 5Access event (card UID, timestamp, temperature) is automatically recorded.

7.2 Temperature Excursion Fail-Safe Lock

Critical

Never attempt to force the locking latches open when the Red LED is active. The fail-safe lock protects patient safety. Forcing it open may result in serious disciplinary action and regulatory violation.

If temperature exits 2°C–8°C for more than 5 continuous minutes: the Red LED illuminates, the RFID system enters DENY mode (3 short beeps, no unlock), and the excursion event is permanently logged. Only an authorized supervisor with a Master RFID card + USB software can clear the lock.

Emergency Procedures

Emergency Decision Tree

Use this tree to determine the correct response when an abnormal condition is detected during a delivery trip.

8.1 Battery Failure / Supercapacitor Activation (Flashing LEDs)

  • 1Do not open the Device — every opening accelerates temperature rise.
  • 2Call your supervisor immediately.
  • 3If a charged replacement battery is available, replace it following Section 9.3.
  • 4If unavailable: return to the nearest facility with a power outlet immediately.
  • 5If unreachable within 20 minutes: transfer to the nearest cold storage location.

8.2 Red LED — Temperature Excursion Protocol

Critical

When the Red LED activates, the vaccines inside are potentially compromised. Under no circumstances should they be delivered to patients until cleared by a qualified authority.

  • 1Stop delivery immediately.
  • 2Contact your supervisor. State your location and time of Red LED activation.
  • 3Secure the Device in the coolest available location. Do not attempt to open it.
  • 4The quality officer will use Master RFID card + USB software to review the temperature log.
  • 5Only the quality officer has authority to declare vaccines safe or compromised.
  • 6If compromised: follow facility waste disposal protocol and document in an incident report.
  • 7Device must undergo full biomedical inspection before returning to service.

Maintenance & Care

Maintenance Schedule

Regular maintenance ensures consistent performance and extends the service life of the Device.

9.1 Daily Maintenance (Before Each Use)

  • Inspect the outer HDPE casing for cracks, impact damage, or deformation.
  • Check the silicone lid seal for tears, debris, or compression damage. Clean with a dry cloth if needed.
  • Verify battery charge level — minimum 80% before deployment.
  • Confirm LED panel responds correctly when powered on.
  • Test RFID card unlock function.
  • Inspect PCM packs for leaks or damage before freezing.

9.2 Weekly Maintenance

  • Clean interior foam holders with 70% isopropyl alcohol wipe — allow to dry completely.
  • Clean the RFID reader panel with a dry lint-free cloth.
  • Inspect all power cables and connections for wear or fraying.
  • Download and backup the temperature log data to the facility server via USB.
  • Test both Peltier modules — verify interior drops below 8°C within 20 minutes at 25°C ambient.
  • Inspect heat sink fins — clear any dust with compressed air.
  • After 5 minutes of operation, carefully touch each Peltier hot-side heat sink for 1 second — should be very hot but bearable. If untouchable, fan has likely failed; power off and contact biomedical technician immediately.
  • Inspect thermal paste at Peltier-to-cold-block interface — if paste appears cracked, dried out, or discoloured, flag for biomedical technician to re-apply before next deployment.
  • Inspect heat pipe evaporator ends inside the box for condensation buildup or physical damage. Heat pipes are sealed and require no maintenance, but if a pipe feels warm at the evaporator end during operation the wick may be degraded — remove from service.

Temperature Data Logging & Reporting

Continuous Audit Trail

Every 5 minutes, temperature, battery voltage, and RFID access events are logged — creating a complete, tamper-evident record for JFDA compliance.

10.1 Automatic Logging

The onboard microcontroller records both DS18B20 sensor readings, battery voltage, and RFID access events at 5-minute intervals throughout every trip. Data is stored in non-volatile memory for up to 30 days, retained even when the battery is removed.

10.2 Downloading Temperature Logs

  • 1Connect USB cable (Type-A to micro-USB, supplied) from your laptop to the USB Data Port.
  • 2Power on the Device.
  • 3Open the TempLogger PC software (supplied on USB drive or from your facility IT officer).
  • 4Select the Device from the connected devices list and click "Download Log".
  • 5Save as CSV. Naming convention: DEVICE-ID_YYYY-MM-DD_TripNumber.csv
  • 6For JFDA submissions, use "Export Report" to generate a PDF.

10.3 Regulatory Requirements

  • Records must be retained for a minimum of 5 years.
  • Available for JFDA inspection within 24 hours of request.
  • Linked to specific delivery batches and vaccine lot numbers in the facility system.
  • Reviewed monthly by the facility quality officer.

Appendix C — Glossary of Terms

BMS
Battery Management System — electronics that protect the lithium-ion battery from overcharge, over-discharge, and thermal runaway.
Cold Chain
The series of temperature-controlled environments and logistics that maintain vaccines at the required temperature from manufacturer to point of use.
EPI
Expanded Programme on Immunization — WHO's global initiative to ensure all people receive recommended vaccinations.
HDPE
High-Density Polyethylene — the outer shell material selected for its low thermal conductivity, chemical inertness, and impact resistance.
JFDA
Jordan Food and Drug Administration — the national authority that enforces standards for vaccine storage and transport.
PCM
Phase Change Material — a substance that absorbs/releases heat during phase transition (solid-to-liquid), enabling stable temperature maintenance without active power.
Peltier Module
A thermoelectric device that creates a temperature difference across its two faces when DC current is applied — used for active cooling in this Device.
RFID
Radio Frequency Identification — a wireless contactless authentication technology. The RC522 module reads 13.56 MHz smart cards.
Supercapacitor
A high-capacity energy storage device providing burst power quickly — used as emergency backup with longer cycle life than secondary batteries.
Temp. Excursion
As defined by WHO: any exposure of vaccines to temperatures outside the approved storage range of 2°C–8°C.
TEC1-12706
The specific Peltier thermoelectric module model used in this Device — rated for 12V, 6A operation.
WHO
World Health Organization — sets international standards for vaccine cold chain management, to which this Device is designed to comply.
